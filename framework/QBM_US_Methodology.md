# QBM-US Methodology

**Version:** 1.21  
Adopted: 13 August 2026  
Status: **Versioned** — amendments are made directly by investor instruction and recorded in the Changelog below. No formal Operational Change Register is required (see [QBM-US Investment Charter](QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#governance-philosophy) for why this differs from QBM).

Governing authority: This methodology is subordinate to the [QBM-US Investment Charter](QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md). If a future instruction conflicts with the Investment Charter, the Investment Charter prevails unless intentionally amended.

Starting ruleset: v1.0 carries forward the process, gates, scoring approach, allocation logic and decision framework of QBM's frozen methodology (as it stood 13 August 2026), adapted only where the ASX-specific target market, benchmark holdings and amendment history do not apply to a fresh US-market system. It is not a fork of QBM's document — it is a new document that starts from the same rules.

## Default commands

The following commands trigger the complete US QBM-US funnel:

- Run QBM-US Full Scan
- Run the complete US QBM-US funnel
- Find the best overlooked US QBM-US candidates
- Run Discovery, Mini QBM and Full QBM on the US market

## Target market

Default universe: US-listed equities (NYSE and NASDAQ), subject to the Universe Filter below. The first scan should still state its actual source list (e.g. S&P 500, Russell 1000, a broader screener) explicitly rather than implying full-market coverage.

## Universe Filter (added v1.1)

Applied before Discovery, to keep low-quality, untradeable and data-poor tickers out of the funnel rather than filtering them out one-by-one during research. This mirrors what QBM itself does in practice on the ASX side (it scans a "top-300 liquid proxy," not the full exchange) rather than trying to genuinely cover the entire market.

A candidate must clear all of the following to enter Discovery:

- **Market capitalisation:** ≥ US$300 million (raised from micro/nano-cap territory; lowered from the original ≥$2B floor in v1.4 — see Changelog). Excludes micro/nano-caps, where data quality, audit rigor and public disclosure are typically weakest, while admitting genuine small-caps.
- **Average daily dollar volume:** ≥ US$5 million. Ensures a position could realistically be entered and exited.
- **Share price:** ≥ US$5. Excludes penny-stock-adjacent names, which carry disproportionate manipulation and data-quality risk.
- **Primary listing:** NYSE or NASDAQ. Excludes OTC/pink-sheet tickers.
- **Security type:** common stock only. Excludes SPACs (pre- and post-merger shells), warrants, rights, units and preferred shares. Foreign private issuers with an NYSE/NASDAQ-listed ADR are included, since the ADR itself trades as an ordinary listed share.

This is a mechanical, objective pre-filter — it does not judge business quality. Business quality, moat, valuation and all other Company Quality questions are still decided at Discovery/Mini/Full QBM as usual; this filter only keeps genuinely untradeable or data-poor tickers from consuming research effort.

Every scan must disclose how many tickers were excluded by this filter and why, the same as any other coverage limitation.

## Quantitative Pre-Screen (added v1.2)

Applied after the Universe Filter and before genuine Discovery-stage research begins. It exists because clearing the Universe Filter still leaves far more tickers (e.g. 1,313 from the first S&P 1500 pass) than can be genuinely, evidence-based researched in one Discovery pass — QBM's own ASX Discovery scan covered roughly 300. Rather than cutting the source universe arbitrarily or pretending to review companies that were never actually looked at, this step applies QBM's own Investment Principles (durable cash generation, high returns on capital, conservative balance sheets, a business that isn't shrinking) as numeric, mechanical proxies.

A candidate must clear all of the following to advance into Discovery:

- **Profitability:** trailing net margin > 0%.
- **Free cash flow:** positive (> $0).
- **Return on equity:** ≥ 10%.
- **Revenue growth (year-over-year):** ≥ 0% — not shrinking.
- **Liquidity:** current ratio ≥ 1.0.

**Health Care sector exemption (added v1.4):** the profitability, free cash flow and ROE gates above do not apply to companies in the Health Care sector. The liquidity gate (current ratio ≥ 1.0) still applies. Clinical-stage and early-commercial biotech is frequently and legitimately unprofitable while building real, evidence-supportable quality (pipeline, IP, partnerships) — applying a profitability screen before any research happens was excluding this entire category, including most oncology-focused names, regardless of underlying quality. This does not weaken Company Quality assessment; it only stops the mechanical pre-screen from rejecting development-stage Health Care candidates unresearched.

**Electronic Technology / Technology Services partial exemption (added v1.9):** the net-margin and ROE gates do not apply to companies in these GICS Information Technology sub-industries: Application Software, Systems Software, IT Consulting & Other Services, Internet Services & Infrastructure, Data Processing & Outsourced Services, Technology Hardware Storage & Peripherals, Communications Equipment, Electronic Equipment & Instruments, Electronic Manufacturing Services. **Free cash flow (> $0) and the liquidity gate (current ratio ≥ 1.0) still apply — this is a narrower exemption than Health Care's, deliberately.**

The investor asked to relax the screen for these sub-industries "like we did with biotech." The justification is not identical, and this Methodology records that difference rather than papering over it: clinical-stage biotech is *structurally* pre-revenue by design — it cannot be FCF-positive before a product is approved, no matter how good the underlying science is. A software or electronic-technology company at $300M+ market cap generally can choose to be FCF-positive; when one deliberately isn't, that is normally a growth-investment decision (heavy S&M/R&D spend ahead of GAAP profitability) rather than a structural impossibility. Net margin and ROE penalise that choice unfairly — a fast-growing, still-scaling software business can show a GAAP loss while its underlying economics are excellent — so those two gates are dropped for this sub-industry set. FCF and liquidity are kept, because a company in this sector that cannot even self-fund is a different, more concerning signal than the same profile in biotech, not an equivalent one.

**Semiconductors and Semiconductor Equipment are deliberately excluded from this exemption's sub-industry list** — both are already the primary target of the Thematic Exclusion (v1.3, AI-industry exposure) and would mostly be screened out again at Discovery stage regardless.

**AI-exposure screening applies to this exemption exactly as it already does everywhere else:** any candidate surfaced under this partial exemption is still subject to the Thematic Exclusion (v1.3) at Discovery stage — material AI-infrastructure revenue or earnings exposure excludes a candidate regardless of GICS sub-industry. "Electronic Technology / Technology Services" is a sector-classification carve-out for the profitability gate only; it is not a carve-out from the AI exclusion.

This is coarse and imperfect by design — it will miss genuine turnaround stories with temporarily weak numbers, and it cannot see moat, management quality or recurring-revenue character, which remain qualitative Discovery/Mini/Full QBM questions. It is a pre-screen, not a substitute for Company Quality assessment.

Every scan must disclose how many tickers were excluded by this pre-screen and why, the same as the Universe Filter.

## Required workflow

1. Reference the active Standing CMEA; create an Event Brief only when material new macro evidence warrants testing whether a replacement version is required
2. US Discovery Scan
3. Mini QBM on candidates that genuinely warrant advancement
4. Full QBM on the strongest finalists
5. Portfolio Challenge Test against current holdings and cash (no fixed benchmark companies — QBM-US starts with no holdings; compare finalists against whatever is actually held or, while the portfolio is 100% cash, against cash itself)
6. Full downstream synchronisation: Company Database, Investment Committee Dashboard, Investment Universe, Master Dashboard, Relative Rankings, Review Calendar, Decision Journals, CMEA Register and Archive, Record Integrity Register, Metadata Completion Register and all operational-intelligence registers

## Discovery requirements

- Start with the broadest verifiable investable US source universe available, then apply the Universe Filter and Quantitative Pre-Screen above.
- Disclose the source universe, number considered, data date, filters (including the Universe Filter's and Quantitative Pre-Screen's exclusion counts), exclusions and coverage limitations.
- Never claim complete US-market coverage unless every relevant listed security was genuinely reviewed.
- Permanently exclude new discovery candidates principally involved in weapons manufacturing, gambling, or tobacco.
- Do not exclude unfamiliar, unfashionable or out-of-portfolio sectors merely for those reasons.
- Apply preliminary Eligibility filters without producing full reports at Discovery stage.
- Produce a ranked longlist of approximately 30–50 companies, or fewer when evidence does not support that many.
- **Longlist depth widening (added v1.17):** when a completed Discovery scan is revisited, extend the longlist to **1.5x its original depth** — the next-ranked tier by the same quantitative ranking composite, using the identical source universe, Universe Filter and Quantitative Pre-Screen already applied — rather than treating the original cutoff as permanent. The ranking composite is a rough, mechanical proxy (ROE/margin/growth), and a company placed just outside an arbitrary top-N cutoff is not meaningfully different from one placed just inside it; widening the depth on revisit catches genuine candidates the first pass's cutoff had no principled reason to exclude. First applied to the Tech Services scan (36 → 54) — see [screens/QBM_US_Discovery_2026-08-20_TechServicesExpanded.md](../screens/QBM_US_Discovery_2026-08-20_TechServicesExpanded.md).

## Mini QBM requirements

- Normally review approximately 15–30 candidates, determined by evidence rather than quota.
- Assess Eligibility, preliminary Company Quality, Investment Attractiveness and Portfolio Fit.
- Record decision, priority, confidence, expected-return range, allocation range, strongest evidence, contradicting evidence and advancement decision.
- Rank candidates by likelihood of improving the portfolio without implying precision unsupported by evidence.
- Reference the active Standing CMEA as a separate evidence overlay to Investment Attractiveness, Portfolio Fit, Review Priority, Implementation Readiness and Monitoring Priorities. Display Current Macro Reference, Decision Impact and Executive Committee Comment without reproducing the complete CMEA. Do not let it directly change Company Quality or independently create a recommendation.
- **Mini QBM Red-Flag Adverse-Event Scan (added v1.18):** before recommending Advance to Full QBM, run one targeted search for undisclosed litigation, SEC/securities investigations, a major guidance cut, or a large single-day price break (e.g. "[ticker] lawsuit", "[ticker] SEC investigation", "[ticker] guidance cut") — lighter than the Full QBM Evidence Verification Standard's three-part pass, but a real, dedicated search, not skipped or folded into general evidence-gathering. Any hit downgrades the recommendation from Advance to Continue Monitoring pending Full QBM's fuller pass, disclosed explicitly, even when operating metrics are strong. Added directly because of a real, demonstrated gap: RAL's $1.4B goodwill impairment (disclosed 4 Feb 2026) and CVLT's 31% single-day crash (27 Jan 2026) were both public and indexed well before the 19 Aug 2026 Mini QBM pass on Tech Services ran, yet Mini QBM's lighter research missed both — only Full QBM's dedicated adverse-event search caught them, a day later and after real research time had already been spent building conviction on both names. A one-query red-flag check at Mini QBM would have caught both at the point they should have been caught. This does not replace the Evidence Verification Standard's fuller three-part pass at Full QBM — it exists to stop a clearly broken thesis from consuming a full Full QBM research cycle before anyone looks for the obvious problem.

## Full QBM finalist requirements

- Select approximately 3–10 finalists only when quality and evidence justify advancement.
- Apply the permanent Executive Committee Summary and complete Research Appendix.
- Compare every finalist directly with current holdings (or cash, while none are held).
- Preserve the decision process and record methodological difficulties only as Validation Observations.
- Display Current Macro Reference, Decision Impact and Executive Committee Comment. Link to the Standing CMEA for Current Macro Impact, reason, time horizon, confidence and detailed portfolio effects rather than reproducing the full assessment.
- **Entry-Point Discipline (added v1.6):** per the [Investment Charter's Entry-Point Discipline](../framework/QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#entry-point-discipline-added-v12-19-august-2026), every Full QBM conclusion must disclose the candidate's position in its 52-week range and its trailing 6-month and 12-month price return, and must state plainly whether the identified quality/catalyst is still ahead of the price or already substantially reflected in it. A name that has already run hard is not automatically rejected, but Implementation Readiness must reflect a stretched entry explicitly (e.g. Await Better Entry) rather than defaulting to Ready.
- **ATH Proximity Rule (added v1.11, widened v1.12):** per the [Investment Charter's ATH Proximity Rule](../framework/QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#ath-proximity-rule-added-v14-20-august-2026-widened-same-day), before any Implementation Readiness is set to Ready or Ready with Considerations, perform a direct current-price-vs-52-week-high-and-all-time-high check as its own step — not derived from a trailing-return calculation. A price within **10%** of either high is a hard floor: Await Better Entry, regardless of confidence or catalyst strength. This is a mandatory, mechanical pre-check, performed before and separately from the broader Entry-Point Discipline disclosure above.
- **Target Holding Period (added v1.5):** per the [Investment Charter's Target Holding Period](../framework/QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#target-holding-period), every Full QBM conclusion must state a specific, evidenced reason to expect the thesis to converge (re-rate, realise a catalyst, or otherwise close the value gap) within approximately 6-18 months — not merely that the business is durably excellent indefinitely. Where no such near/medium-term convergence reason exists, state this explicitly as a weakness even if Company Quality is high; a high-quality business with no plausible 6-18 month catalyst is a weaker fit for QBM-US than one with a specific, evidenced reason to expect realisation in that window. Risks whose primary impact horizon is materially beyond 18 months (e.g., a 2030s patent cliff) should be disclosed but weighted proportionately less than risks that could disrupt the thesis within the target window.
- **Insider Ownership & Net Buying Screen (added v1.13):** a numeric proxy for the Charter's "prefer high-quality management" principle, in the same spirit as the Quantitative Pre-Screen's proxies for cash generation and capital allocation. Two gates, both required for an Add decision:
  1. **Minimum combined officer & director ownership, tiered by market-cap tier** (same $2B/$10B breakpoints used in Position Sizing): Mega/Large-cap (≥$10B) ≥0.5%; Mid-cap ($2B-$10B) ≥2%; Small-cap ($300M-$2B) ≥5%. Tiered because insider ownership is mechanically a percentage of market cap — a mega-cap CEO can hold a fortune in absolute dollar terms and still show under 1%, so a flat threshold would silently exclude every mega-cap regardless of actual management alignment. The gradient exists precisely so this doesn't happen.

     **Near-miss provision (added v1.16):** ownership at or above **80% of the applicable tier threshold** (≥0.4% mega/large-cap, ≥1.6% mid-cap, ≥4% small-cap) no longer fails outright. It passes as a disclosed, discounted pass — Decision Confidence pulled down at least one level and Priority capped at Tier 3, the same mechanism already used for material net selling — rather than being hard-blocked. Below 80% of the threshold remains a hard fail; this graduates the edge of the gate, it does not remove it. Added for the same reason Gate 2 was softened: applying the tiered thresholds to the 15 Tech Services candidates found KN (1.7% vs. a 2% mid-cap bar), ARLO (4.03% vs. 5% small-cap) and PLXS (1.78% vs. 2% mid-cap) all failed by a margin that reads as noise in a single-source ownership figure, not as a genuine absence of insider alignment — the same false-precision problem the ownership data itself already has elsewhere in this project (see the AGYS price-conflict and DLB/CNXN ownership-data-gap findings). A hard cliff at an exact percentage was treating a noisy number as if it were exact.
  2. **Net insider selling — disclosed and weighted, not a pass/fail gate (demoted from a hard gate, 20 August 2026).** The original hard "net buying required" version failed all 15 companies it was tested against; softening it to "no material net selling" still failed 13 of 15, mostly for ordinary executive life-administration selling (diversification, taxes on vested RSUs, pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans) rather than anything that actually signalled a problem. Insider *selling* is simply too common and too often benign to function as a reliable binary filter — unlike insider *buying*, which is rare enough that it means something when it happens, or ownership *level* (Gate 1), which is a stable structural fact rather than a noisy transaction stream. Blocking an Add on selling patterns risks rejecting good businesses for reasons unrelated to quality far more often than it catches real problems, and in the two cases where selling genuinely mattered (see below), Full QBM's ordinary adverse-event research already caught the real story without needing a mechanical gate.

     Net selling is still assessed and disclosed for every Full QBM candidate, using the same materiality test built for the softened gate — but it now moves **Decision Confidence and Priority Tier**, not the Investment Decision itself:
     - **Excluded from the calculation entirely:** tax-withholding "surrender to issuer" transactions tied to RSU/option vesting, and routine small plan-based purchases (ESPP) — mechanical, not discretionary.
     - **Material selling** (same tests as before: tiered net-dollar thresholds — ≥$2M small-cap / ≥$5M mid-cap / ≥$15M mega-large-cap over 6 months; any single insider reducing their holding by >10%; or multiple C-suite officers selling in a concentrated window) should pull Decision Confidence down at least one level (e.g., Medium-High → Medium) and cap Priority at Tier 3, stated explicitly as the reason — not silently absorbed into a generic confidence rating.
     - **Selling into the company's own disclosed adverse event** (after a guidance cut, a stock-price collapse, or amid active litigation) is a materially worse signal than the same dollar amount of routine selling and should be named as such.
     - Immaterial or absent selling is a neutral fact, not a positive one — it does not itself raise confidence.

  3. **Insider buying — disclosed positive factor (added v1.20).** The symmetric counterpart to Gate 2: genuine, discretionary open-market insider buying over the trailing 6 months should raise Decision Confidence and/or Priority Tier, stated explicitly, the same mechanism already used for material selling but in the other direction. This was part of the original v1.13 design intent but had no operative effect once Gate 2 was demoted — buying was tracked for the ownership-increase test, never separately credited as a positive signal in its own right. **Excluded from the calculation, same as Gate 2:** RSU/option vesting, tax-withholding transactions, and routine plan-based purchases (ESPP) — mechanical, not discretionary. Because genuine open-market buying is rare (the original v1.13 test found essentially none across 15 real companies), this will affect few names in practice — that scarcity is exactly why it should count for something when it does happen, the same reasoning that already makes buying "rare enough that it means something" in Gate 2's own rationale above. Immaterial or absent buying is a neutral fact, not a negative one — it does not itself lower confidence; a company is not penalized simply because no insider happened to buy.

     **Retroactive check (20-21 August 2026 evidence):** re-reading the insider-transaction detail already gathered for the 24 standing Adds (no new research performed) found exactly one genuine discretionary buy: **KN (Knowles)** — a single ~$423K open-market purchase, alongside the ~$9M in selling that already keeps KN at Medium-Low confidence. Disclosed as a partial offset, not a reversal: the buy is real but dwarfed roughly 20x by the same-period selling, so it tempers rather than changes KN's existing confidence rating. No other standing Add showed genuine buying in the data already on record. **The original 6-holding portfolio's core names (LLY, MA, VCYT, PTCT, XNCR, ACAD) were assessed before the Insider Ownership & Net Buying Screen existed and have never had insider-transaction detail gathered at all** — this rule inherits that same open gap already disclosed for Gate 1/Gate 2 above, not a new one.

  **Gate 1 (ownership tier) remains a hard pass/fail gate below the near-miss band** — it did not have the same false-positive problem Gate 2 had, since ownership level is stable and structural rather than a noisy transaction stream; the near-miss provision above only softens the edge of the gate, it doesn't remove the gate itself. Requires per-company lookup, not bulk screener data — a Full QBM check, the same way the Evidence Verification Standard's adverse-event check is. A company with unconfirmed ownership data should not be assumed to pass; state the data gap explicitly and withhold the Add decision until it's resolved, consistent with never inventing missing data. **Not yet applied retroactively** to Add decisions made before v1.13 — see the Methodology Changelog for the verification gap this creates on the standing target portfolio.

- **Evidence Staleness Re-verification Requirement (added v1.21):** any company holding a slot in the target portfolio whose Full QBM evidence is more than **30 days old** must complete a lightweight re-verification pass — a repeat of the Evidence Verification Standard's three-part search (current price, adverse event, analyst sentiment) plus a fresh ATH Proximity Rule and Valuation Ceiling check — before any resizing decision, replacement comparison, or renewed Implementation Readiness claim is treated as current. This is lighter than a full Full QBM re-run (Company Quality and Investment Attractiveness are not re-derived from scratch unless the re-verification surfaces something material) but heavier than doing nothing between investor-prompted reviews, which has been the default until now. Motivated directly by this session's own evidence: SCSC's price moved from $59.60 to $52.08 in one day, and VSH/IPGP's confirmed crashes were found only because a scan happened to run that day, not on any schedule — a live, sized target-portfolio position sitting on stale evidence is a different risk than a bench candidate in the same position, precisely because capital is meant to actually move on it. This does not apply to bench candidates, which are re-verified only if and when they're being considered for a slot. **As of 21 August 2026, no target-portfolio holding exceeds the 30-day threshold** — the oldest evidence (LLY, MA) was substantively re-verified 19-20 Aug (Target Holding Period and Entry-Point review); the first re-verification trigger will fall in mid-to-late September 2026.
- **Valuation Ceiling (added v1.19):** per the [Investment Charter's Valuation Ceiling](../framework/QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#valuation-ceiling-added-v16-20-august-2026), every Full QBM conclusion must disclose current price against the average analyst consensus 12-month price target as an explicit percentage premium or discount, sourced the same way the Evidence Verification Standard's price check already is. No Implementation Readiness of Ready or Ready with Considerations is permitted when current price sits **more than 20% above** that average target — a hard floor, checked directly, the same mechanical way the ATH Proximity Rule is checked, and independent of it (a name can clear the ATH check and still fail this one, or vice versa). Where analyst coverage is too thin or absent for a meaningful consensus (common among small/mid-cap and early-commercial biotech), state this explicitly as a data gap rather than forcing a number — the ceiling then simply does not apply, it is not treated as a pass.

## Position Sizing Framework (added v1.7, replaced v1.8)

Every prior Allocation range was asserted by judgment, not derived — the Open Items list below has said "No position-sizing or order-execution logic defined" since v1.0. v1.7 first closed that gap with continuous market-cap-tier bands (8-15% / 4-8% / 2-4%). **v1.8 replaces that with the investor's explicit Portfolio Concentration instruction** (see [Investment Charter](../framework/QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#portfolio-concentration-added-v13-19-august-2026)): 5-8 holdings, no position below 10%, sized in three discrete tiers.

**The three tiers:**

| Tier | Size | Fit |
|---|---:|---|
| Core conviction | 20% | Highest-confidence Add, diversified thesis (not resting on one binary event), deep liquidity |
| Standard conviction | 15% | Solid Add with real evidence behind it; may carry one disclosed open item (an unresolved litigation status, a stretched entry) but the thesis itself is multi-driver |
| Minimum-inclusion | 10% | Genuinely Add-rated, but either the near-term thesis depends materially on a single binary event, or the company carries some other real, disclosed caveat that argues against sizing it any larger — included at the floor rather than excluded, when the investor wants exposure anyway |

**Selecting which 5-8 of the standing Add list actually get a slot** is now a required, explicit step — Add alone no longer implies inclusion in the funded portfolio, because a 10%+ position is a materially bigger commitment than the old 2-6% bands ever were. Full QBM's Investment Decision (Add / Continue Monitoring / etc.) is unchanged by this — Portfolio Construction is a separate, subsequent step that selects and sizes from the standing Add list, the same way Implementation Readiness and Position Sizing are already kept separate from Investment Decision.

**The binary-catalyst disclosure requirement carries forward unchanged in substance**, but its consequence is sharper under concentration: a company whose Add thesis leans on a single dated binary event (FDA decision, pivotal readout, litigation ruling) must be flagged as such at the point of portfolio selection, not just at Full QBM — a 10-20% position riding on one event outcome is a fundamentally different bet than the same size in a diversified compounder, and the Charter now requires this be stated plainly rather than averaged into a generic "Add."

**What this does not do:** as before, it does not fold Implementation Readiness into the sizing math — sizing answers "how much," readiness answers "when." It also does not yet define an explicit ranking rule for which names win the 5-8 slots when more than 8 companies are Add-rated (currently 10 are) — that selection is made by disclosed judgment (Decision Confidence, diversification of thesis, and binary-catalyst risk) each time it's needed, recorded in a dedicated portfolio-construction record, not silently.

**Portfolio-level guardrail (unchanged in spirit, restated in the new terms):** no single position above 20%, none below 10%, and QBM-US shall not force a full 8 holdings or force capital fully invested merely to fill slots — cash remains an acceptable residual when fewer than 8 names clear the bar for 10%+ conviction.

## Portfolio Challenge Test

- Compare current holdings, finalists and cash on Company Quality, Investment Attractiveness, Portfolio Fit, confidence, expected return, allocation range, decision and relative rank.
- Identify the strongest and weakest holdings, strongest outside candidate, best diversifier, best expected return, best downside protection, expensive quality companies and insufficiently proven opportunities.
- Require a demonstrably superior, evidence-supported and downside-aware expected return before recommending replacement.
- Determine the Investment Decision from Company Quality, Investment Attractiveness, Portfolio Fit, concentration, correlation, evidence confidence and downside-aware expected return.
- Record tax, brokerage, transaction costs, current weights, liquidity, cash, sizing and execution timing separately under Implementation Readiness and Implementation Notes; these inputs must not dilute the ownership conclusion.
- When no candidate clearly passes the hurdle, conclude: **No replacement justified.**

## Accuracy and progressive coverage

- Prioritise audited accounts (10-K/10-Q), SEC filings, earnings releases, and independently verifiable operating data.
- Grade secondary evidence and never invent missing data.
- When the workload exceeds what can be properly evidenced in one pass, complete and report each stage progressively rather than implying false completeness.

## Evidence Verification Standard

QBM-US has no live market-data feed or financial-terminal connection. All evidence comes from web search, which is sensitive to how a query is phrased and does not self-verify. General evidence-gathering searches can miss a more severe or more current characterisation of a known issue even when that information is publicly available and indexed at the time.

Before any Full QBM finalist conclusion is finalised, run a dedicated final-verification search pass, separate from general evidence-gathering, consisting of at minimum:

1. A direct current-price query (e.g. "[ticker] share price today"). Where sources disagree and cannot be reconciled, disclose the range rather than asserting a single figure, and prefer an investor-supplied live quote over search-derived figures when one is available.
2. A direct adverse-event query (e.g. "[ticker] recall", "[ticker] lawsuit", "[ticker] SEC investigation", "[ticker] safety"), aimed explicitly at surfacing the most severe available characterisation of any known issue rather than stopping at the first plausible result.
3. A direct analyst-sentiment query (e.g. "[ticker] analyst downgrade", "[ticker] price target cut", "[ticker] rating change"), covering at minimum the trailing 6 months — not just since the prior scan's evidence cut-off.

Disclose the verification search timestamp — not just the calendar date — in the output. If verification could not be completed before publishing, state this explicitly rather than implying completeness.

## Ethical exclusions

Permanently exclude new discovery candidates principally involved in:

- weapons manufacturing;
- gambling; or
- tobacco.

Where exposure is indirect, incidental or ambiguous, identify it clearly before treating the company as eligible.

## Thematic exclusions (added v1.3; crypto exclusion added v1.10)

Distinct from the ethical exclusions above — see [UserProfile.md](../UserProfile.md#thematic-exclusions-distinct-from-ethical-exclusions) for the full statement and rationale.

**AI-industry exposure:** exclude candidates with material revenue or earnings exposure to the AI-infrastructure buildout — applied broadly (AI chip/software companies, semiconductor-equipment suppliers to AI chipmakers, and hyperscalers whose capex or cloud revenue is substantially AI-driven), not narrowly to pure-play AI companies only.

**Crypto exposure (added v1.10):** exclude candidates with material revenue, earnings or balance-sheet exposure to cryptocurrency — applied broadly, matching the AI exclusion's pattern: crypto miners and crypto-mining infrastructure/hosting providers, crypto exchanges and brokerages, and companies holding cryptocurrency as a material treasury asset, not narrowly to pure-play crypto-asset issuers only.

Both are investor risk-view preferences, not permanent ethical exclusions — either may be revisited if the investor's view changes, unlike the ethical exclusions above. Apply both at Discovery stage going forward so exposed candidates are screened out before consuming Mini/Full QBM research effort, the same way the Universe Filter and Quantitative Pre-Screen already narrow the funnel mechanically. Where exposure to either theme is indirect, partial or ambiguous, identify it clearly and disclose the judgment call rather than silently including or excluding the company — the same discipline already applied when a company was swept in by GICS sector classification alone (see the Tech Services scan's CleanSpark finding) rather than genuine business exposure.

## Operational maturity control

Do not propose methodology additions because they appear theoretically attractive. Every future change should arise from a real investment decision, validation finding, recurring operational weakness or demonstrated data-quality issue — but unlike QBM, it does not need a formal Operational Change Register entry to take effect. Record it as a new version below with a one-line reason.
