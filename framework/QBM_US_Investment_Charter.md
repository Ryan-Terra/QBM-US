# QBM-US Investment Charter

**Version:** 1.6  
**Status:** Permanent  
**Date Adopted:** 13 August 2026  
**Last Amended:** 20 August 2026 (Valuation Ceiling added — see [Amendment History](#amendment-history))

## Purpose

This Investment Charter defines the permanent philosophy of the Quality Before Momentum — US (QBM-US) Investment Operating System.

It exists to protect the integrity of QBM-US over decades.

It is the highest governing document within QBM-US and sits above every methodology document, operating standard, dashboard, report and assessment.

Where a future proposal conflicts with this Investment Charter, the Investment Charter prevails unless it is intentionally amended.

QBM-US is an independent operating system. It shares its founding philosophy and document architecture with the original QBM Investment Operating System (ASX-focused) but has no shared git history, holdings, methodology version or governance decisions. Amending one system never amends the other.

## Mission

QBM-US exists to maximise long-term after-tax wealth by consistently allocating capital into exceptional US-listed businesses purchased at attractive prices while avoiding permanent capital loss.

The objective is not to predict markets.

The objective is not to outperform every quarter.

The objective is to make consistently better long-term investment decisions through disciplined, evidence-based research.

## Core Philosophy

Quality comes before momentum.

Businesses create wealth.

Markets merely price them.

Share-price movements do not create business quality.

Temporary market volatility is an opportunity when business quality remains intact.

Investment decisions should be driven by evidence rather than emotion.

## Primary Objective

The primary objective of QBM-US is to own the highest-quality portfolio that can reasonably be constructed using the available evidence.

The objective is not to own the greatest number of companies.

The objective is not to maximise trading activity.

The objective is to continuously improve portfolio quality while minimising unnecessary turnover.

## Investment Principles

QBM-US shall:

- Prioritise exceptional businesses.
- Seek durable competitive advantages.
- Prefer recurring revenue.
- Prefer strong cash generation.
- Prefer excellent capital allocation.
- Prefer high-quality management.
- Prefer conservative balance sheets.
- Require sufficient evidence before committing capital.
- Maintain a margin of safety.
- Target a 6-18 month holding period per position (amended v1.1 — see [Target Holding Period](#target-holding-period)), while compounding wealth across many such positions over years.
- Accept temporary volatility within a position's target holding period in exchange for the thesis playing out; volatility that extends materially past the 18-month upper bound without evidence of continued convergence is itself a signal to reassess, not a reason for indefinite patience.

## Risk Philosophy

Risk is defined primarily as the probability of permanent capital loss.

Short-term volatility is not, by itself, risk.

Market declines are expected.

Economic cycles are expected.

Macro events are expected.

Only permanent deterioration in business quality justifies abandoning a long-term investment thesis.

## Macro Philosophy

The Current Macro Environment Assessment (CMEA) provides context.

It does not replace company analysis.

The Standing CMEA exists to answer:

> What world are we investing in today?

QBM-US then answers:

> What businesses should we own in that world?

Macro conditions may influence:

- Investment Attractiveness
- Portfolio Fit
- Review Priority
- Monitoring

Macro conditions alone shall not determine Company Quality.

## Evidence Philosophy

Every investment conclusion must be supported by evidence.

Evidence is preferred over opinion.

Primary sources are preferred over secondary sources.

Uncertainty must be acknowledged.

Unknown information must never be invented.

Where evidence is insufficient, QBM-US shall state that evidence is insufficient.

## Decision Philosophy

Every recommendation shall answer two separate questions:

1. What should the portfolio own?
2. How should that decision be implemented?

Investment quality and implementation logistics shall remain permanently separate.

## Portfolio Philosophy

The portfolio is managed as one integrated system.

Every holding competes continuously for its place.

No company owns a permanent position.

Replacement decisions shall be based on whether a new opportunity improves the entire portfolio.

### Portfolio Concentration (added v1.3, 19 August 2026)

QBM-US targets **5-8 holdings**, sized in three discrete tiers — **10%, 15% or 20%** of the portfolio — with no position held below 10%. Investor-directed: a position too small to matter is a distraction, not a diversifier; QBM-US shall hold fewer, larger, higher-conviction positions rather than spreading capital across marginal ideas at a size too small to move the portfolio.

This is a deliberate trade against textbook diversification, and the Charter states the trade-off it creates rather than hiding it: a single-name loss in a 5-8-holding, 10-20%-sized portfolio has materially more effect on total portfolio outcome than the same loss would in a more diffuse portfolio. QBM-US's existing discipline — sufficient evidence before committing capital, a margin of safety, avoiding permanent capital loss — carries more weight under concentration, not less, and Company Quality and Investment Attractiveness must be held to that bar precisely because position sizes are large.

A specific consequence for Investment Attractiveness: a company whose near-term thesis rests on a single binary event (an FDA decision, a pivotal trial readout, a litigation ruling) carries a materially different risk character at a 10-20% position size than a diversified, multi-driver business does at the same size — a single adverse outcome can impair a much larger share of total capital. QBM-US shall disclose this distinction plainly for every holding and prospective holding; it does not by itself bar a binary-catalyst company from the concentrated portfolio, since the investor may knowingly choose to size such a position at the 10% floor rather than exclude it, but the concentration this size implies must never be understated or averaged away.

## Governance Philosophy

QBM-US methodology is **versioned, not frozen**. This is a deliberate departure from the original QBM system, adopted because QBM-US exists specifically to allow faster rule iteration without touching QBM's governance.

Every methodology change is recorded as a new version with an inline changelog entry in the [QBM-US Methodology](QBM_US_Methodology.md) stating what changed and why. There is no formal Operational Change Register requirement — the investor may authorise a rule change directly.

Methodology shall never evolve merely because a new idea appears attractive; each change should still be traceable to a reason. But the bar for making that change is deliberately lower than QBM's.

## Historical Preservation

QBM-US is an append-only operating system.

Historical assessments are never overwritten.

Decision Journals are permanent.

Standing CMEAs are permanent.

Committee Minutes are permanent.

Portfolio Timeline entries are permanent.

Historical decisions shall remain visible even when later proven incorrect.

## Validation Philosophy

QBM-US shall continuously evaluate itself.

Validation exists to measure the quality of decisions rather than justify them.

Evidence gathered through validation may improve the operating system.

Validation shall never rewrite history.

## Integrity

The operating system shall:

- State assumptions clearly.
- Separate facts from opinions.
- Disclose uncertainty.
- Preserve historical records.
- Avoid hindsight bias.
- Avoid narrative-driven investing.
- Avoid emotional decision making.
- Remain internally consistent.

## Long-Term Horizon

QBM-US as an operating system is designed to operate over decades — but this describes the system's persistence and the compounding of results across a long sequence of positions, not the intended holding period of any single position. See [Target Holding Period](#target-holding-period) for the position-level horizon.

Temporary market conditions shall not compromise the system's long-term discipline in finding and correctly sizing quality opportunities.

Compounding is expected to create the majority of long-term wealth, achieved by repeatedly identifying exceptional businesses at attractive prices, realising the thesis within its target holding period, and redeploying capital into the next opportunity — not by holding any individual company indefinitely.

Patience is a competitive advantage within a position's target holding period. It is not a justification for holding a position materially beyond that window without fresh evidence that the thesis is still converging.

## Target Holding Period (added v1.1, 19 August 2026)

QBM-US targets a **6-18 month holding period per position**, set at initiation and revisited on evidence, not on a fixed calendar date. This is a deliberate, investor-directed departure from typical "buy and hold for years" long-term-compounding strategies (including QBM's own ASX approach), adopted specifically for QBM-US.

This does not change what QBM-US buys — Company Quality still requires durable competitive advantage, strong cash generation, excellent capital allocation and a conservative balance sheet, exactly as before. It changes two things:

1. **Investment Attractiveness must identify why value is likely to be recognised or converge within roughly 6-18 months** — a specific catalyst, valuation re-rating case, or evidenced near/medium-term inflection — not merely that the business is durably excellent over a multi-decade horizon. A wonderful business with no plausible reason to re-rate or realise value within that window is a weaker fit for QBM-US than it would be for a genuinely indefinite-horizon strategy.
2. **Risks that only matter on a multi-year-or-longer horizon** (for example, a patent cliff a decade out, or gradual multi-year competitive erosion) **carry proportionately less weight** in the Investment Decision than risks that could plausibly disrupt the thesis within the target holding window. They should still be disclosed — QBM-US does not hide long-horizon risk — but they should not, by themselves, be treated as disqualifying the way they would under a true buy-and-hold-for-years framework.

Quality Before Momentum is unchanged as the security-selection filter: momentum (price action, sentiment, hype) is still never a substitute for evidenced business quality. What has changed is realisation horizon, not selection criteria — QBM-US still buys quality, it simply now expects to sell it, not hold it forever, once the thesis has played out or the 6-18 month window has run without evidence of continued convergence.

Position reviews should occur at least every 3 months per position, with a mandatory re-underwrite at the 6-month mark (is the thesis still on track to converge within the target window?) and a hold/exit decision required by the 18-month mark absent fresh evidence extending the thesis.

## The Investment Committee

The Investment Committee exists to challenge ideas rather than confirm them.

Every recommendation should be capable of withstanding rigorous scrutiny.

Disagreement improves decision quality when supported by evidence.

Consensus shall never replace evidence.

## Quality Before Momentum

Quality Before Momentum means:

- Find exceptional businesses.
- Understand them deeply.
- Wait patiently.
- Act decisively.
- Monitor continuously.
- Validate honestly.
- Repeat indefinitely.

### Entry-Point Discipline (added v1.2, 19 August 2026)

Quality Before Momentum cuts both ways. QBM-US shall not chase a quality business after the market has already re-rated it — a great business bought after it has already broken out and run hard is a momentum purchase wearing a quality label, not a Quality Before Momentum purchase.

Investment Attractiveness must therefore explicitly assess **where a candidate sits in its own recent price history**, not only where the price sits relative to an analyst target:

- How far the current price sits from its 52-week high and low.
- Trailing 6-month and 12-month price return, disclosed plainly rather than only cited when favourable.
- Whether the price move already reflects the thesis (the quality is priced in) or the thesis is still ahead of the price (the market has not yet recognised it).

A business that has already risen sharply is not automatically disqualified — durable quality can keep compounding from a higher price, and a real near-term catalyst can justify paying up. But QBM-US shall disclose this plainly as a distinct factor, and shall prefer, all else equal, a candidate whose quality is not yet fully reflected in its price over one whose quality has already driven a large run. Where a name has already run hard, Implementation Readiness should reflect that explicitly (e.g. Await Better Entry) rather than folding a stretched entry price silently into an unqualified Ready.

### ATH Proximity Rule (added v1.4, 20 August 2026; widened same day)

Added after a real error: XNCR was rated Ready on 19 August 2026 based on a trailing-return figure that was wrong — the underlying data conflict was even noticed at the time and not chased down before publishing. A trailing-return calculation pulls from multiple, sometimes inconsistent sources and is exactly the kind of number that can be gotten wrong quietly. A direct proximity check against the 52-week high and all-time high is simpler, uses one number each, and is harder to get wrong the same way — so it becomes a mandatory, mechanical floor, not just a disclosure.

**QBM-US shall not rate a position's Implementation Readiness as Ready — or Ready with Considerations — when the current price sits within 10% of its 52-week high or its all-time high, whichever is lower.** This applies regardless of Decision Confidence, Company Quality, Priority Tier or how compelling the near-term catalyst is. It is a hard floor, not a factor to be weighed against others.

This check must be performed directly (a current-price query against 52-week-high and all-time-high figures) as its own step, separate from and prior to any trailing-return calculation — the proximity check is the primary gate; trailing return is supporting disclosure, not a substitute for it.

**Threshold history:** set at 5% on first adoption (20 August 2026), widened to 10% the same day on direct investor instruction ("5% isn't enough"). Neither version is an immutable constant — either may be revisited by direct investor instruction, the same way every other numeric threshold in this Methodology can be. The threshold change did not alter the outcome for the current target portfolio: MA (4.26% below its all-time high) and XNCR (0.5% below its 52-week high) both already failed the 5% band and remain Await Better Entry; PTCT (17.0% below its 52-week high) clears both the 5% and the 10% band and remains Ready with Considerations. The practical effect of widening to 10% is on candidates not yet assessed — a materially larger band of "too close to the top to call Ready" going forward.

### Valuation Ceiling (added v1.6, 20 August 2026)

The ATH Proximity Rule checks a position against its own price history. It does not check a position against what the market's own analyst community currently thinks it is worth — a stock can sit well below its 52-week high and still be priced meaningfully above where informed third-party opinion puts fair value. Both are real, independent ways for Quality Before Momentum to be violated at the entry point, and QBM-US shall check both, separately.

**QBM-US shall not rate a position's Implementation Readiness as Ready — or Ready with Considerations — when the current price sits more than 20% above the average analyst consensus 12-month price target.** This is a hard floor, checked directly (current price against the average consensus target, sourced and dated the same way the Evidence Verification Standard's price check already is), independent of the ATH Proximity Rule — a candidate can clear one check and fail the other, and both must be disclosed regardless of outcome.

Where analyst coverage is too thin, too new, or too dispersed for a genuine consensus to exist — common among small/mid-cap and early-commercial biotech names, where a handful of analysts can produce wildly different targets built on different clinical-trial-outcome assumptions — QBM-US shall state this explicitly as a data gap rather than force a number. The ceiling does not apply when no genuine consensus exists; an absent check is not treated as a pass.

This rule was adopted alongside, not instead of, a relaxation elsewhere in the same Methodology revision (the Insider Ownership Screen's near-miss provision and the Discovery longlist depth-widening rule) — QBM-US tightens and loosens different parts of its funnel independently, based on what the evidence from real application actually shows, not as a package deal that must net to the same overall strictness.

## Charter Amendment

This Investment Charter is intended to remain stable over time.

Amendments should be rare — the Charter defines philosophy, not tunable rules. Rule tuning belongs in the versioned Methodology, not here.

A Charter amendment requires clear evidence that the change materially improves long-term investment decision quality while remaining consistent with the mission and philosophy of QBM-US. Because QBM-US is investor-directed and versioned by design (unlike QBM's frozen governance), a clear, explicit investor instruction to change the Charter's stated investment horizon or philosophy itself satisfies this bar directly — the evidence required is the investor's own stated intent for their capital, not a separate empirical justification.
