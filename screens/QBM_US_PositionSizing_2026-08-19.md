## What actually moved, and why it's not a rubber stamp

Two results are worth naming because they cut against intuition:

**HQY's range went up, not down, despite the confirmed data breach.** The instinct is to shrink a position when bad news lands. But under this framework, breach risk is company-specific risk, and company-specific risk decides *where in the tier band* a position sizes, not whether it can breach the tier's structural floor — that floor exists because of HQY's size and liquidity, which the breach hasn't changed. The breach is already expressed twice elsewhere: Implementation Readiness (Await Better Entry) and elevated Review Priority. Sizing it below the mid-cap floor as well would be double-counting the same risk a third time.

**XNCR's range went down, despite being one of the three "Ready" names in the Entry-Point Discipline review.** A well-timed entry and an appropriately-sized position are different questions. XNCR being priced before its re-rating (per the 19 Aug Entry-Point review) is a reason to be willing to buy it *now* rather than wait — it is not, by itself, a reason to size it *large*. The position is still a small-cap name whose near-term thesis leans on a single binary clinical readout, and that risk character caps the size regardless of how good the entry looks this week.
