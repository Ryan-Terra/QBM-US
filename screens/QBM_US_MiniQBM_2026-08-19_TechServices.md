## AI-exposure Eligibility check — three more exclusions found during Mini QBM research

Discovery-stage screening only caught PATH (UiPath) on an obvious, named strategic repositioning. Company-level Mini QBM research — reading what each company's own Q2 2026 results and guidance actually attribute growth to — surfaced three more, using the same test the 14 Aug general scan already established: **does revenue/earnings depend on selling into the AI-infrastructure buildout (chips, data centers, hyperscaler capex), not merely "AI" as a marketing descriptor for an unrelated product feature.**

| Ticker | Company | Why excluded | Why this is different from names that were kept in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CLSK** | CleanSpark | Explicitly pivoting from bitcoin mining toward AI/HPC data-center infrastructure — 1.8GW of contracted, grid-secured power capacity, active hyperscaler lease negotiations. This is a direct AI-infrastructure buildout business, not adjacent to it. | — |
| **ATEN** | A10 Networks | Company's own Q2 2026 results and raised guidance are explicitly attributed to "AI-related traffic, latency and security requirements" and "AI-driven networking and security requirements" — its growth is a function of customers building AI infrastructure. | Contrast with QLYS's "AI-native Risk Operations Center" — that's AI as a feature Qualys built into its own product, sold into an unrelated (cybersecurity) end-market. QLYS stays eligible; ATEN does not. |
| **ADEA** | Adeia | Raised its long-term revenue target specifically citing "hybrid bonding adoption in memory and logic chips scales the semiconductor unit as AI buildouts grow" — its semiconductor IP-licensing segment is explicitly and quantifiably tied to AI chip manufacturing techniques. | Its media-licensing segment (the majority of current revenue) is not AI-related, but the company's own stated forward growth case leans on the semiconductor/AI-buildout segment specifically. |

**Two names with "AI" language that were reviewed and kept eligible**, for contrast and to show this wasn't an automatic keyword trigger:
- **CALX** ("AI-native platform"): describes AI/ML built into Calix's own software for broadband operators — a product capability, not exposure to customers' AI-infrastructure capex.
- **FIVN** ("AI revenue accelerating"): AI-powered contact-center agents sold as a feature within customer-service software — the same pattern, not AI-infrastructure exposure.
