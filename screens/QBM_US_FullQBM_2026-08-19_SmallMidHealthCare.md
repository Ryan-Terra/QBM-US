## HQY — HealthEquity, Inc.

**Company Quality:** High on business fundamentals (dominant HSA custodian, 17M+ accounts, legislative eligibility-expansion tailwind) — **but this pass's dedicated adverse-event search surfaced a confirmed, material data breach not known when this name advanced at Mini QBM.**

**Adverse-event finding — material and previously unknown to this system:** HealthEquity confirmed in an SEC 8-K filing that a business partner's device was compromised, exposing the personal identifying information of **4,300,000 individuals**. A class-action lawsuit has been filed alleging negligence, negligence per se, breach of contract and unjust enrichment. For a company whose entire business model depends on being trusted to custody sensitive health and financial account data, a confirmed breach of this scale is a direct hit to the specific thing the business sells — not an unrelated external event, the same category of concern the Methodology already treats seriously when it shows up at cybersecurity vendors (see FTNT, rejected 14 Aug 2026 for exactly this reason).

**Valuation:** $102.79 current. Analyst target $118.53 (Strong Buy, ~15% upside) — but this figure's sourcing predates confirmation of how the market and regulators will price in the breach, so should be treated cautiously rather than taken at face value.

**Near-term catalyst (6-18mo):** the HSA-eligibility legislative expansion remains real, but is a steady multi-year tailwind, not a sharp event — the same weaker-fit pattern flagged for HQY in the Target Holding Period review is still only partially addressed.

**Portfolio Fit:** Health Care (functionally financial-services-adjacent — HSA custody).

**Decision: Add, but materially more cautious than the Mini QBM record implied.** **Priority:** Tier 3 (down from Tier 2). **Implementation Readiness:** Ready with Considerations — the breach is the dominant near-term risk to monitor (litigation cost exposure, potential regulatory action, customer-trust/churn risk); do not size a position before at least one further evidence check on the breach's scope and any regulatory response. **Confidence:** Medium (down from Medium-High). **Expected-return range:** 3-18% (widened and lowered versus what the pre-breach analyst target alone would suggest). **Allocation range:** 2-4% (reduced).

With that search now surfacing an open regulatory investigation and thin analyst-implied upside (~5.6%), the margin of safety is too thin to justify committing capital, regardless of otherwise-strong fundamentals. This is a textbook case for why the Evidence Verification Standard exists as a mandatory, separate, final pass — not a formality.

**Decision: Continue Monitoring (revised from Advance to Full QBM).** **Priority:** Tier 3. **Confidence:** Medium — revisit once the California DOI investigation resolves and/or a clearer valuation margin develops.

## INVA — Innoviva, Inc. — **downgraded from Advance to Continue Monitoring**

**Company Quality:** Genuine near-term earnings drivers — recurring strategic-investment fair-value gains, IST segment growing 40-50% annually, stable GSK royalty base.

**Adverse-event check:** Clean. No lawsuit or SEC investigation found for 2026.

**Valuation — genuine, credible bipolar analyst disagreement, the decisive factor:** $21.25 current. Buy consensus (5 analysts) averages $32.8 (~54% implied upside) — but Goldman Sachs specifically **initiated coverage at Sell with a $17 target** (~-20% implied downside), citing the 2031 royalty patent cliff. This is not ordinary target dispersion; it's two credible, opposing theses on fair value from sophisticated analysts. On top of that, a meaningful share of INVA's recent earnings strength comes from **mark-to-market gains on strategic investments** ($157.7M + $33.6M in Q1 2026 alone) — by nature volatile, non-guaranteed income, not demonstrated recurring cash flow.

**Why this changes the decision:** the Target Holding Period review correctly discounted the 2031 patent-cliff risk as outside the target window — that reasoning still holds. But Full QBM's valuation work surfaces a separate, more immediate problem: the current price sits in the middle of a genuine bull/bear split, and a meaningful part of the "strong near-term earnings" case rests on non-recurring investment gains rather than operating cash flow. The Charter requires "sufficient evidence before committing capital" — a coin-flip-level credible analyst disagreement on fair value, layered on earnings quality that's partly mark-to-market, doesn't clear that bar yet.

**Decision: Continue Monitoring (revised from Advance to Full QBM).** **Priority:** Tier 3. **Confidence:** Medium-Low — genuinely unresolved evidence, not a weak case either way.

### A concentration issue this Portfolio Challenge Test must name explicitly

If all 8 new Adds were funded alongside the existing LLY and MA Adds, the portfolio would hold **10 Add-rated positions, 9 of which are Health Care** (only MA is outside the sector), and **8 of those 9 are small/mid-cap** (LLY is the only mega-cap Health Care holding). QBM-US's Investment Principles don't require sector diversification, and each of these 8 names individually clears the bar against cash on its own evidence — but a portfolio built from all 8 simultaneously would carry real, correlated exposure to sector-wide risk (health-policy shifts, biotech-sector sentiment swings, sector-wide funding conditions) that no single-company Company Quality assessment captures. This is a Portfolio Fit observation for the investor to weigh at position-sizing/execution time, not a reason to reject any individual name — but it should not be left unsaid.
