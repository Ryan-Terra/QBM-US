# QBM-US Concentrated Portfolio Construction — 19 August 2026

Triggered by direct investor instruction: 5-8 holdings, no position below 10%, sized in 10/15/20% tiers — "go hard or go home." Recorded in [Investment Charter v1.3](../framework/QBM_US_Investment_Charter.md#portfolio-concentration-added-v13-19-august-2026) and [Methodology v1.8](../framework/QBM_US_Methodology.md#position-sizing-framework-added-v17-replaced-v18).

**Why this is a separate, required step now:** at the old 2-8% bands, every standing Add could reasonably get some size. At 10-20% minimum, that's no longer true — 10 companies are Add-rated but only 5-8 can hold a slot without either exceeding 100% or forcing sizes below the 10% floor. This document makes that selection explicitly, with reasons, rather than funding the first 6-8 alphabetically or by Tier.

## Selection principle

Two things earn a slot in a 10-20% position: (1) real Decision Confidence, and (2) a thesis that doesn't rest primarily on a single binary event — because at this size, a single adverse outcome (a failed trial, a rejected FDA application) impairs a much larger share of total capital than it would in a diffuse portfolio. Per the Charter, a binary-catalyst company is not automatically excluded, but it must be sized at the 10% floor and flagged plainly, never averaged into a generic "Add."

## Binary-catalyst disclosure (Charter-required, not optional)

**25% of this portfolio (XNCR + ACAD) has a near-term thesis component that depends on a single dated event.** If either Phase 3 dose data (XNCR) or the Phase 2 readout (ACAD) disappoints, that position could re-rate sharply downward independent of everything else going right in the portfolio. This is disclosed plainly, not netted against the portfolio's other strengths: a 15% and a 10% position each riding partly on one binary outcome is a real, live risk in a 6-holding book, not a footnote.

## Sector concentration — now sharper than before

**5 of 6 holdings (85% of the portfolio) are Health Care / pharma-biotech; only MA (20%) sits outside.** Concentrating to 6 names did not reduce this — it sharpened it, because the sector concentration flagged earlier (9 of 10 Add-rated names were Health Care) has now been compressed into fewer, larger positions rather than spread across ten smaller ones. A single sector-wide shock (drug-pricing policy, an FDA-wide slowdown, biotech-funding conditions tightening) would now hit 85% of the book, not a diversified fraction of it.
