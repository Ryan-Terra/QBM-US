# QBM-US ATH Proximity Rule — 20 August 2026

## The error that triggered this

On 19 August 2026, XNCR was rated **Ready** in the Entry-Point Discipline review, based on a recorded trailing-year return of −18.3%. That figure was wrong. The same table entry noted, in the same breath, that the current price ($22.89 at the time) sat *above* the stated 52-week high ($21.26) — a direct internal contradiction — and flagged it as "range likely stale." The contradiction was never resolved before the Ready conclusion was published and fed into the concentrated-portfolio construction.

Re-verified 20 August 2026: XNCR trades at $21.89, within 1% of its actual 52-week high ($21.99), up **178.71%** over the trailing year — comparable to NEO (+170%/yr), which had been correctly flagged as the most extreme already-broken-out name in the batch. XNCR should have been graded the same way.

The investor's response was not to ask for that one number to be fixed — it was to ask for a rule that stops this failure mode from recurring.

## Why a proximity check, not a better trailing-return calculation

Trailing-return figures are computed from a starting price a year (or 90 days, or whatever window) ago, pulled from a source that may be stale, mislabeled, or simply wrong — exactly what happened here. A **proximity check against the 52-week high and all-time high** uses one current number against one reference number, both of which are easy to state and verify directly, and hard to get quietly wrong the way a computed percentage can be. It is a blunter instrument, but a more reliable one for the specific job of catching "is this already at its peak."

## The rule (Investment Charter v1.5 — widened from v1.4's original 5% the same day)

**QBM-US shall not rate a position's Implementation Readiness as Ready — or Ready with Considerations — when the current price sits within 10% of its 52-week high or its all-time high, whichever is lower.** Hard floor, not a weighed factor. Applies regardless of Decision Confidence, Company Quality, Priority Tier, or catalyst strength.

The proximity check must be performed as a direct, standalone step — a current-price query against the 52-week-high and all-time-high figures — before any trailing-return calculation, not after. Trailing return remains required disclosure (Charter v1.2, Entry-Point Discipline), but it is no longer the primary gate.

**Threshold history:** set at 5% on first adoption; widened to 10% the same day (20 August 2026) on direct investor instruction — "5% isn't enough." Neither number is fixed — revisable by direct investor instruction like any other numeric threshold in this Methodology. The widening did not change today's outcomes (see below): MA and XNCR were already well inside 5%, and PTCT clears both bands with room to spare. Its effect is on candidates not yet assessed.

## What this means practically

This does not change the target portfolio's composition or weights — MA 20%, LLY 15%, VCYT 15%, PTCT 15%, XNCR 15%, ACAD 10% stands, because Position Sizing was never based on Implementation Readiness (the Charter has kept these separate throughout). What it changes is execution timing: **there is currently no holding in the 6-name target book that can be bought today without paying within 10% of an all-time or 52-week high, except PTCT**, which remains Ready with Considerations (its own separate open items — litigation status, a conflicting analyst target — still apply).

This is a genuinely more conservative, more disciplined position than QBM-US was in 24 hours ago, and it is a direct, intended consequence of the rule the investor asked for — not a malfunction of it. A concentrated, 10-20%-per-position portfolio built from a longlist of names that already screened well on growth and quality metrics will tend to include names that have already run; a hard proximity floor is what actually enforces "before it breaks out," rather than a preference stated once and then not mechanically checked.
